    About

    Named after a Latin word meaning ‘I estimate’, Aestimo is a powerful tool for translation teachers which makes checking students' translations faster, easier, and more accurate. First of all, open the editor to provide the programme with the source text, the list of students, and their translations. Then press the button in the main menu to begin checking the works. The check itself is designed to be performed on a paragraph-by-paragraph basis, that is working with one source paragraph and all of its translations at a time instead of dealing with whole texts. This simplifies translations comparison and mistake search (don't hesitate to try out the feature to automatise the process!) and thus lowers the risk of missing something important. Apart from that, the programme automatically calculates the resulting marks, while taking into account the source text's length using a special multiplier1.

    Classification of mistakes

    1. Type:
      • Grammar: a mistake while conveying the meaning of a grammatical form (a wrong tense, article, declension, etc.).
      • Vocabulary: a mistake while conveying the meaning of a word (a wrong word, collocation, style, etc.).
      • Orthography: a wrong spelling of a word.
      • Punctuation: an incorrect usage of punctuation marks.
      • Complex: a mistake, usually a serious one, in a text unit larger than a character or a word (e.g., a phrase or a sentence).
    2. Subtype:
      • Redundant element: a wrong extra element is inserted.
      • Incorrect element: a correct element is replaced with a wrong one.
      • Lack of element: a correct element is absent.
    3. Text's meaning:
      • Retained: the mistake does not make understanding the text harder.
      • Obscured: the text's meaning can be understood only with the help of the immediate context2.
      • Lost: the text's meaning is severely distorted and cannot be understood even with the help of the immediate context2.
    Notes
    1. Text length multiplier = 1800 / total number of characters in the original text.
    2. Immediate context = everything before the analysed sentence + this sentence + one sentence after it.
    3. Repeated mistake counts as one mistake.
